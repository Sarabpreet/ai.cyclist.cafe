CYCLIST CAFÉ × MCP

Your café.
Your assistant.
One conversation.

Why Cyclist Café has an MCP server,
how to use it, and when it helps.

A short introduction for customers, the café team and developers

Open with a familiar question: what if you could check the café menu in the assistant you already use? This presentation explains the intended experience and the approval steps that keep the customer in control. This is an introduction, not a claim that the hosted MCP connection has completed production rollout.

Why we built it

A café question should be
easy to ask

“What’s on the menu?
Can I take it away?
What’s happening at the café?”

The idea: let a familiar conversation reach current café information.

People start with questions, not endpoint names. The purpose is to make menu discovery, takeaway planning and event participation available inside a compatible AI assistant. We are offering an additional way to reach the café, while the existing customer and staff systems continue to serve their roles.

Why we built it

Answers should come
from the café

The assistant
understands the request.

The café supplies the menu, listed options, prices and availability.

The customer checks the details before any order or RSVP is confirmed.

No invented products, prices, offers or reservations.

An assistant can phrase a recommendation, but it must consult the live tools for facts. Descriptions and example conversations are not proof of stock. A quote does not reserve stock, and an event link does not reserve a seat. Availability is checked again when the customer confirms.

What MCP does

A common connection
for AI assistants

MCP means Model Context Protocol.

YOU ASKYour assistant→TOOLS CONNECTCyclist MCP server→THE CAFÉ ANSWERSMenu · Orders · Events

Works with clients that support remote MCP, subject to their account permissions.

MCP gives compatible assistants a standard way to discover and call tools. Cyclist exposes a selected set of café operations through that boundary. It is separate from staff login and is not a general-purpose staff administration interface. For the protocol, see https://modelcontextprotocol.io/docs/develop/connect-remote-servers.

How to connect

One server address

  1. Add a remote MCP connectionUse ChatGPT, Claude or another compatible client.
  2. Connect your customer accountApprove only the permissions you want to use.
  3. Select Cyclist Café and askExact setup controls depend on your client and account.

Step-by-step client instructions → ai.cyclist.cafe/#connect

Use the exact HTTPS address including /mcp. ai.cyclist.cafe is the guide, while api.cyclist.cafe/mcp is the server. Ordering and RSVP use customer OAuth. The server supports dynamic client registration (DCR), not CIMD. Consult the linked guide and official client help for current account-specific setup. Do not paste passwords, OTPs or bearer tokens into a chat.

How to order

A takeaway in four steps

  1. ChoosePick a current item, its options and quantity.
  2. Review the quoteCheck the priced bill, taxes and any valid saved coupon.
  3. Open the Cyclist linkVerify your identity and approve the exact order.
  4. Collect and pay at the caféTrack the submitted order through your assistant.

A prepared checkout link is not a submitted order. Quotes expire and do not hold stock.

The sequence is get_cafe_status, search_menu, get_menu_item, quote_order and prepare_checkout. The customer opens the returned trusted URL and confirms. The assistant uses get_checkout_status and get_my_order to track the result. This MCP flow supports takeaway with counter payment; it does not collect an online payment.

How to RSVP

Choose your response.

Going requests a place.
Interested records interest without reserving a seat.

Approve

RSVP links expire after ten minutes. Capacity is checked at confirmation.

Published events are discovered using list_events and get_event. prepare_event_rsvp creates the customer-reviewed request. Going is subject to capacity; Interested holds no place. Cancellation is also a newly prepared and approved action. A confirmation link itself does not hold a seat. Staff check-in remains in the staff module.

A new way to ask.
The same café operations.

MCP does not give customers access to staff actions, cash records or other people’s information.

Staff continue using their existing roles and screens. Customer confirmation is the business boundary that submits the order or applies the RSVP. The MCP connection does not let a customer mark a bill paid, adjust inventory, perform check-in or inspect staff or other customer data. Existing web and mobile authentication remain separate.

Identity and control

Your account.
Your final say.

  1. Verify on CyclistEnter phone verification details on the trusted customer page.
  2. Approve specific permissionsBrowsing, quoting, checkout and RSVP have separate boundaries.
  3. Disconnect when you wantRevoke the assistant connection from the customer page.

Never share an OTP, password or access token in the assistant’s chat.

The connected customer is linked to the existing People profile; registration is required for RSVP. Revoking the assistant blocks future token access and unconfirmed actions, but does not erase or cancel an already approved order or RSVP. For those, use the supported cancellation process or contact the café.

When to use another channel

Some requests need
the café directly

Use the café / existing app

Dine-in table requests, delivery,
online payment, loyalty redemption
and changes to submitted orders.

Speak to staff

Allergies, uncertain ingredients,
urgent help and unusual requests.

Dietary tags and AI recommendations do not guarantee allergen safety.

This slide describes the documented MCP flow, not every feature of the wider POS or customer app. The server currently exposes selected takeaway and event operations. Listed dietary tags can help discovery but should never substitute for direct allergy advice from staff. Do not invent substitutions that are absent from current menu options.

If something changes

Check the result
before trying again

  1. A link expired or an item changedAsk for a fresh quote or RSVP request.
  2. An event filled upCheck availability again before preparing another response.
  3. A confirmation timed outCheck order or RSVP status first; avoid duplicate submissions.

A link or an assistant message alone is not proof that an action completed.

Tools can report business errors even when the HTTP response is 200. The assistant must inspect the tool outcome. RSVP retries for the same intended command reuse the same UUID; a changed response gets a new one. A checkout request with the same quote can reuse its prepared link. Never confirm again blindly after a timeout.
